Mission and uncertainties

SUAVE simulates an autonomous underwater vehicle (AUV), a BlueROV2 running ArduSub, that must find and inspect a pipeline on the seabed. The AUV starts close to where the pipeline is expected to be, but does not know its exact location, as in a real deployment.

Mission

The mission is a sequence of tasks:

  1. A1: Search for the pipeline. The AUV follows a spiral path at a configurable altitude until its camera detects the pipeline.

  2. A2: Follow and inspect the pipeline. Once the pipeline is detected, the AUV follows it and inspects as much of it as possible.

The ROSA extension adds a third task:

  1. A3: Recharge the battery. When the battery runs low, the AUV interrupts its current task, recharges, and then resumes the mission.

The default mission is time-constrained: it ends after a time limit (time_limit, 300 s by default), whether or not the pipeline was found.

Uncertainties

While the AUV operates, its environment and its own components change in ways that are not known at design time:

Uncertainty

Effect

Adaptation

U1: Thruster failures

One or more thrusters stop working during A1 or A2. The motion controller still commands them, so the AUV deviates from its path

Activate thruster recovery (f_maintain_motion in fd_recover_thrusters). Recovery always succeeds

U2: Changing water visibility

During A1, the camera cannot detect the pipeline when the AUV searches above the water visibility

Select a lower or higher spiral altitude (1, 2, or 3 m) for the current visibility

U3: Low battery

During A1 or A2, the battery level reaches a critical threshold

Interrupt the current task and run A3, which needs its own architectural configuration

U1 and U2 only require changing the architecture, and the task sequence stays the same. U3 also changes the task sequence. The scenario with U1 and U2 is the original use case, and the scenario with all three is the extended use case. Water current is available as an optional environmental disturbance (enable_water_current), with no built-in adaptation.

The disturbances are simulated and configurable:

  • Thruster failures are scheduled through the thruster_events parameter of thruster_monitor. Each entry (thruster, failure|recovery, delay_s) names a thruster (1 to 6) and the seconds since the previous event.

  • Water visibility varies periodically between a minimum and a maximum, by default from 1.25 m to 3.75 m with an 80 s period.

  • Battery level drops linearly over discharge_time (200 s by default). A level below battery_limit (0.25) counts as low battery.

The experiment runner can shift these disturbances randomly between runs. See Run SUAVE for the campaign parameters.

Search altitude trade-off

A higher search altitude gives the camera a larger footprint, so the AUV covers the search area faster. But the pipeline is only detected when the altitude is below the water visibility. The expected altitude for each visibility is:

The thresholds are the water_visibility_threshold and expected_altitude parameters of mission_metrics, which uses them to measure reaction times.

Key performance indicators

The mission’s success is measured with two KPIs:

  • Search time: how long the AUV takes to find the pipeline.

  • Distance inspected: how much of the pipeline the AUV inspects before the time limit.

SUAVE also measures the adaptation reaction time: how long the managing subsystem takes to adapt after a disturbance. See Metrics Reference for how each metric is recorded.

Quality requirements

SUAVE’s quality requirements are written in RELAX: each one has a fulfillment degree between 0 (violated) and 1 (fully satisfied), instead of simply passing or failing.

Requirement

Statement

Thrusters availability

The AUV SHALL keep the number of operational thrusters AS CLOSE AS POSSIBLE TO the number required by the active motion controller

Search footprint

The AUV SHALL observe AS MUCH of the search area AS POSSIBLE while searching for the pipeline

Adaptation reaction time

The AUV SHALL complete an appropriate adaptation AS EARLY AS POSSIBLE AFTER a thruster failure, a water visibility change, or a low battery

Each requirement captures a stakeholder concern without prescribing a solution. For example, thrusters availability can be satisfied by recovering the failed thrusters, or by switching to a motion controller that needs fewer thrusters.

The requirement_monitor node publishes the fulfillment of each requirement at runtime on /requirements/<requirement>/fulfillment. Managing subsystems can use these topics to decide when to adapt, and the offline scripts plot them per managing subsystem. The RELAX expressions, fuzzy membership functions, topics, and scripts are described in the suave_requirements README.
